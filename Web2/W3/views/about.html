About

Records of ancient China show that making noodles is older than what we thought. The records go back to 3000 B.C. and this is important because the majority of people believe that Marco Polo introduced noodles to the rest of the world in the 13th century and got all the credit. Although noodles are different from pasta, they made the foundation for creating pasta later on in history. Another possible root of pasta can be found as far back as the 4th B.C., this time in Etruscan civilization, found in a tomb that illustrated a group of people making something that looked a lot like pasta

Arab culture was another factor that had an impact on the creation of pasta because of the Muslim conquests of Sicily in the 8th century. They add durum wheat and other cooking techniques to the picture, which revolutionized the development of pasta even more and to this day, are still in Sicilian pasta recipes. Dried pasta gained popularity in Italy because of its dietary value. Another reason for its popularity was that it didn’t spoil as fast as other foods, which made it a perfect food for sea travel in 1300s, not to mention it was easy to cook and prepare. Finally in 19th century, one of the greatest changes happened to pasta: tomato. By adding tomatoes, pasta dishes became even more popular and delicious

So now that we know about the history of pasta, it is expected to see pasta in all different kinds sizes and shapes and all these attributes have a different story to tell about that kind of pasta and its historical milestone. With different shapes, looks, and sizes, come various sauces and cooking techniques

We talked about pasta in Italy, now it’s time to talk about pasta in America! This exquisite dish was introduced by Italian people who immigrated to America in the late 19th century, and thus pasta tightened its grip. This, combined with pasta’s cooking methods, found its way into America. But the key was not only the taste; Pasta was an affordable dish and as said before, fairly easy to make, so during World War II, it even became more popular among people. This popularity didn’t stop after the war and with things like pre-packaged pasta, it became even more accessible and convenient

Pasta is more than just a food to just eat. It is a symbol of Italian culture, illustrating concepts of family, sharing meals, and Italian traditions. It had a long journey, the journey that it shared with Italy, from Etruscan civilization to the immigration of people of Italy to the new world. Italians enjoy spending time together. One of the things that let them do this, is by making and eating pasta together, not only sharing a meal, but sharing time and precious moments
